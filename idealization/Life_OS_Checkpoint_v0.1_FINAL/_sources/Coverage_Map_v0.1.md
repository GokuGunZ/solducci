## 3. Coverage by major design concern

### Whole-life orientation

Covered by Foundation, Self & Life, Planning/Ceremonies and contextual Views. Strong conceptual coverage; real case-study coverage for health/relationships/travel remains intentionally light.

### Knowledge and learning

Strong coverage through Concept/Knowledge, generic-vs-contextual application, Resources, progressive structuring, derivation and case studies (GDPR/App Mobile, F2L, Crochet).

### Projects and action

Strong coverage through Project identity, Goal/Milestone/Task direction, Phase, Idea promotion and resume. Goal/Scenario/Trajectory representation remains epistemically Working/Open.

### Time and experience

Strong coverage for Session, Metric, Observation, Practice and Temporal Period/Phase distinction. Recurrence/cadence and subjective experience remain deliberately underformalized.

### Self and values

Medium-strong conceptual coverage. Values and Interests are described but not frozen into dedicated types. `Me`/Self as an ontological object remains Open.

### System intelligence

Medium coverage: signals are identified and philosophically constrained, but exact derivation thresholds/algorithms are intentionally deferred.

## 4. Requirement coverage status

- **Strong:** idea preservation, resume, project progress/decomposition, knowledge model, hobby/practice support, ceremonies, planned-vs-actual.
- **Medium-strong:** value/self reflection, knowledge-to-action debt, emerging importance, synergies.
- **Medium:** whole-life map, life-maintenance visibility.
- **Low/needs second-wave cases:** health, relationships, bureaucracy, travel and recurring maintenance behavior.

## 5. Key gaps surfaced by the coverage review

1. **Self as object** - `Me` is used conceptually but not yet ontologically defined.
2. **Literal/raw content** - first step before Property/Entity promotion needs formalization.
3. **Capability formalization** - property->capability->operation is strong as a principle but lacks formal semantics.
4. **Early process candidates** - CODE, Project Development and Area Development were not rejected; they need re-evaluation after primitives are defined.
5. **Maintenance-domain case studies** - health, bureaucracy, relationships, travel and recurring routines are underrepresented.
6. **Formal ceremony outputs** - ceremony questions and roles are clear; exact output objects/relations remain open.
7. **Subjective experience** - feelings/mood/sensation are intentionally loose; later stress tests should verify that this is sufficient.
8. **Artifact** - repeatedly appears as an output but has not been tested as a distinct type vs generic Entity + `produced-by`.
9. **Area** - appears in life/project maps but its ontology is still unclear (responsibility, container, concept, view grouping).
10. **Recurrence/cadence** - central to routines/practice/ceremonies but not yet modeled.
11. **Collaborative ownership/permissions** - present in real projects but deliberately not explored yet.

## 6. Duplication/confusion risks

- Knowledge vs Concept: avoid two duplicate containers.
- Goal vs Scenario: do not freeze independent types before understanding shared composition.
- Phase vs Mode: a Phase may have a Mode; they are not synonyms.
- Phase vs Temporal Period: Established as distinct.
- Ceremony vs Temporal Period: a ceremony occurs in time and can govern a period, but is not the period.
- Observation vs Insight: observation is noticed experience; insight is stronger synthesized understanding.
- Personal Value vs property value: terminology conflict to resolve in ontology.
- Life Area vs Project Area: shared word may hide different semantics.

## 7. Cross-document traceability that must remain available

Three transversal maps are required:

1. **Requirement traceability:** Need/SR -> Model -> Mechanism.
2. **Provenance:** Case Study -> Insight -> Rationale -> Decision.
3. **Principle traceability:** DP -> concepts/mechanisms it constrains.

The dedicated `Traceability_Map_v0.1` materializes these relationships for the archival checkpoint.

## 8. Coverage conclusion

The checkpoint has strong coverage of the conceptual work actually performed. The remaining gaps are known and explicitly preserved rather than accidentally omitted. The corpus is suitable as the input to Ontology Minimum v0.1 provided that maintenance domains are later used as second-wave stress tests.
